Recently, I hit Master Complete on the Voltaic Season 5 benchmarks.

Accomplishing this was a gratifying milestone, as Master Complete was the long-term goal I had set my eyes on back when I first started aim training. In the grand scheme of things, Master Complete definitely isn't the be-all and end-all - leaderboards place it around the top 2-3% across the categories. However, it genuinely feels like I finally "completed the tutorial," and any further progression means stepping into apex-tier territory.

![My Voltaic S5 Master Complete benchmark table](/media/articles/voltaic-s5-master-complete/benchmarks.png)

It was a cool experience to cross this threshold right around the time I turned 30. Having only started aim training at 27, this grind reaffirmed my belief that age really isn't a physical barrier when it comes to acquiring motor skills. If anything, balancing dedicated practice with the mental fatigue of daily adult responsibilities is far harder than getting your hands to move precisely.

Part of what made this climb special to me was doing it "my way" - I alternated between lightweight mice and fast skates on ultra-fast glasspads. I have a decent amount of peripheral experience and know firsthand that my progression was much faster whenever I used a more traditional balanced setup. However, I've always been a bit of a rebel - I enjoy pursuing things my own way, which in this case meant stubbornly sticking to my ultra fast setup. As a result, I can honestly say this was accomplished on my own terms.

Anyhow, I wanted to document these thoughts before the details faded, partly as a personal post-mortem, and partly in case it's useful to anyone staring down a similar path.

## Background

To give some context, my background isn't actually in FPS games but rather League of Legends. On the League side, I was high diamond in China back in season 3 and eventually played/ coached/ managed my university's teams, but from an aiming perspective, my experience was very sparse, mostly from getting carried by a friend who used to play in pro lobbies in CS, brief spurts in Apex around Platinum, and casual Valorant sessions ever since. Aim was always a bit of a weak point for me, and it definitely lingered in the back of my mind throughout the years.

I was loosely aware of Kovaak's for a while—I even remember reading an old Aimer7 guide back in the day—but the real turning point was watching the Red Bull Ready Check event a little over two years ago. It was awesome seeing an entire community built around mechanical improvement that still felt relatively underground.

![The Red Bull Ready Check event](/media/articles/voltaic-s5-master-complete/redbull-ready-check.webp)

And down the rabbit hole I went! I started out on Aim Labs, placing around Gold on the Voltaic S3 benchmarks. I remember being genuinely hooked on the process—I'd leave work every day excited to boot up Aim Labs and actively look up video guides and resources to absorb information from. I hit Platinum shortly after.

Reaching Diamond came after a stretch of consistent grinding, mostly following VDIM as I began picking up aim theory. Around this time, I picked up a SkyPAD 3.0, which added a new learning curve, but my gains started slowing down. I also swapped over to Kovaak's as that seemed to be the more flexible option.

![VoltBot rank progression through Gold, Platinum, and Diamond](/media/articles/voltaic-s5-master-complete/voltbot-progression.png)

Then real life hit. I got laid off and had to pivot almost all my mental bandwidth into technical interview prep. Between staring at code all day and dealing with the drain of interviewing, long sessions just weren't happening. Most of 2025 ended up being an unintended maintenance phase.

Once I finally landed a job and the stress settled, I was able to refocus. I spent some time going through the Viscose S1 benchmarks, rediscovered how much fun Kovaak's could be, and eventually channeled that momentum into the recent push for Voltaic Season 5.

I'm not quite sure what the trigger was, but after floating around Diamond/Jade and mostly casually playing through scenarios in the Viscose benchmarks, I had an idea to make a push for the VT benches around mid-May. Initially, I just wanted to test the waters and see where I could go. I gave the static clicking scenarios a go and ended up hitting Master scores even while trying out fingertip mice. That gave me the confidence to set my sights on Master Complete and commit to a full, concentrated grind through July.

My routine during this period was straightforward if not extreme. I'd target a scenario, start with the related VDIM scenarios to warm up, then continuously grind that scenario until I either hit the score or was too tired to grind.

It was honestly quite shocking how much tolerance I had for longer hours. Previously I would mostly stop at an hour max for a single session (mostly sticking to 20-40 minute sessions), but for some reason this time around, I was able to sit down and repeatedly grind for hours on end.

Is brute-forcing 3-4 hour sessions of a single scenario the optimal way to improve technique? Probably not. But for the first time in a while, I was having genuine fun sitting down and obsessing over the grind.

For the majority of the benchmarks, this approach didn't feel completely random - I could actively feel improvement as I tried to address flaws in my technique. There were only a handful of scenarios where I felt like I was hitting a complete brick wall, which did make the final breakthrough that much sweeter.
